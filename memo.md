# TECHi traffic: decision memo

The public article sample is real; all traffic figures below are invented assessment data. They are compared for context, never joined to assign sessions to articles.

**a. Is traffic up 30% year on year?** We cannot verify that claim. The table contains September 2025–August 2026 only: no same-month prior-year comparator or previous annual total. Reported sessions rise from 57,500 to 102,805: `(102,805 / 57,500 − 1) × 100 = 78.79%` between endpoints, not YoY and not unique-reader growth.

**b. Best estimate of underlying change.** January–February rises from 64,000 to 94,685, or 47.95%. Five section ratios cluster at 1.475–1.491 immediately after the stated site-wide snippet replacement. Given that note, a common measurement break is plausible; the monthly aggregates cannot independently prove its cause or size.

For AI, Markets, Breakthroughs and Policy, forecast February as January plus each section’s median September–January monthly increment. Divide observed February by those forecasts and take the median multiplier: 1.45. Crypto’s temporary spike and Guides’ different trend make them poor primary calibration sections. Apply the multiplier only from February onward. August becomes `102,805 / 1.45 = 70,900`; comparable-session growth is `(70,900 / 57,500 − 1) × 100 = 23.30%`. This assumes a persistent, uniform recording change and continuing pre-change trends. Alternative 0–5% organic-February-growth assumptions give 20.54–26.56%; that is a scenario range, not a confidence interval. Unique-reader growth remains unknown.

November Crypto’s 24,800 sessions compare with 10,100 in October and 11,000 in December. The midpoint baseline is 10,550; possible non-recurring excess is 14,250, or 57.46% of November Crypto. Neighboring-month baselines give 13,800–14,700. Removing that excess changes November and period totals, not endpoint growth. The aggregator explanation is consistent but unverified: we have no November article identity or referral records.

Monthly newsletter signups rise from 820 to 1,140: 39.02%. These are acquisitions, not active subscribers. Without opening subscribers, churn and engagement, subscriber-priced newsletter inventory cannot be valued. Session-priced advertising also needs a validated denominator; no revenue or costs are supplied. Whether section sessions are mutually exclusive is unspecified; totals assume additivity.

**Two findings from the article rows.** Umair Aslam has `5/20 × 100 = 25%` of sampled AI bylines; eight byline labels occur. All 20 AI dates follow August, so this cannot verify the second writer’s September 2025 arrival or contribution. Guides has `18/20 × 100 = 90%` of shown dates after March, including 15 in April–August. This challenges “almost nothing since Mar” on visible dates, but cannot establish historical publishing frequency; dates may reflect updates. October’s newest-20 samples span unequal periods and contain 109 distinct URLs across 120 section memberships.

**c. Retire Guides? No, not yet.** Its March–April sessions fall 55.88%, even within the post-change regime. Audit section tracking, redirects and search visibility; cap a four-week evergreen-refresh pilot at one editor-day. That is a proposed resource limit, not a measured cost. If keeping Guides is wrong, that time displaces higher-return work and the stated near-zero session advertising persists. If retirement is wrong, useful search traffic and newsletter acquisition could be lost; their value is unmeasured.

I would change my mind if... a tracking-validated four-week pilot produced no verified newsletter acquisitions and no recoverable session contribution; I would then merge or retire Guides.

**Next free number:** count session-start analytics events from one clean browser visit to a Guides article and one to an AI article, using browser developer tools. Duplicate or missing events would prioritize the tracking audit; the result would not by itself calibrate historical readership.
